# Correlation and Logical Arbitrage on Polymarket

## Strategy Overview
Logical arbitrage involves exploiting pricing inconsistencies between correlated or mutually exclusive markets on Polymarket. This strategy relies on mathematical certainty rather than directional betting.

## How it Works
1.  **Correlation Arbitrage**: Identify markets where one outcome must imply another.
    *   **Example**: If "Trump wins 2028" is at 35%, then "Republican wins 2028" MUST be at least 35%. If it's lower (e.g., 32%), you buy "Republican wins" at 32%.
2.  **Cumulative Probability Violations**: Find markets where the total probability of all mutually exclusive outcomes exceeds 100%.
    *   **Example**: In a "Which month will recession be declared?" market, the total probability across all months and "No recession" sums to 111%.
3.  **The Trade**: Sell the overpriced outcomes and buy the underpriced ones, letting the market inefficiency correct itself.

## Why it Works in 2026
*   **Retail Inefficiency**: Most retail traders only look at one market at a time and don't check for cross-market consistency.
*   **Bayesian Failures**: Humans are poor at updating beliefs across multiple correlated outcomes.

## Real-World Examples
### The Super Bowl Contradiction
*   **Market A**: "Chiefs win Super Bowl" — 28%
*   **Market B**: "AFC team wins Super Bowl" — 24%
*   **The Trade**: Buy "AFC team wins" at 24%. If the Chiefs win (28% probability), you profit. If any other AFC team wins, you also profit. You literally can't lose unless an NFC team wins.
